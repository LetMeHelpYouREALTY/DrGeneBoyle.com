These 5 research themes changed how I talk about motherhood and mental health.
(Sharing the framework for free)

I'll be honest — most coverage of women with serious mental illness (SMI) only talks about risk.

Custody loss. Poverty. Violence. Homelessness.

Those risks are real.
But they are not the whole story.

Most professionals still miss this:

↳ Women with SMI who choose not to parent. 👀
↳ Women whose parenting plans got derailed. 💔
↳ Women rebuilding what “mother” means on their own terms. 🌱

I went back to the research.

Mizock, Merg, Boyle & Kompaniez-Dunigan (2019) interviewed 20 women with SMI — with and without children — in *Psychiatric Rehabilitation Journal*.

They found five themes:

↳ Motherhood declined
↳ Motherhood derailed
↳ Motherhood disabled
↳ Motherhood reimagined
↳ Mattering through motherhood

That last one matters.
Motherhood isn’t only a risk profile.
For many women, it’s identity. Meaning. Feeling like they matter.

If you support women with SMI — clinically, personally, or in community — here’s the shortcut:

1. Ask about parenting goals without assuming the answer
2. Center self-determination (her values lead)
3. Map the resources she’d need if she wants kids — or wants a different path
4. Make room for grief when plans get derailed
5. Honor “reimagined” motherhood, not only biological parenting

Takes one better conversation.
Not another stigma-heavy checklist.

You could keep talking about women with SMI only through risk.
Or you could use a framework that already holds choice, loss, adaptation, and meaning.
