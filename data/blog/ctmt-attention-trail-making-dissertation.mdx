**Focus (from the title).** The dissertation investigates how useful the CTMT-2 is when clinicians assess attentional deficits in pediatric and adolescent populations—not adult-only or general cognitive screening alone.

## What the CTMT-2 measures

The CTMT-2 is a standardized neuropsychological instrument built around five timed visual search and sequencing “trails.” Performance is heavily influenced by attention, concentration, resistance to distraction, and cognitive flexibility (set-shifting). Common clinical uses include evaluation of brain injury and other central nervous system compromise, frontal-lobe–related deficits, psychomotor speed, visual search/sequencing, and monitoring recovery after concussion or TBI (instrument publisher materials).

Raw completion times convert to T scores (mean 50, SD 10), with composite indices derived from pooled trail scores. Norms for the second edition were drawn from a large multi-state U.S. sample (publisher documentation).
