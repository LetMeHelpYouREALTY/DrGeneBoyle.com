## Abstract (author text)

Boyle, Eugene. (2024). Investigating the Utility of the Comprehensive Trail Making Test -Second Edition (CTMT-2) in Assessing Attentional Deficits Among Children and Adolescents. The accurate assessment of attentional deficits is crucial for the diagnosis and treatment planning of attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) in children and adolescents, and the Comprehensive Trail Making Test-Second Edition (CTMT-2) has shown promise as a tool for evaluating these deficits. The present study aimed to assess the relationships between CTMT-2 performance and cognitive abilities, attentional deficits, and attentional capacities in school-aged children and adolescents (*N* = 30), aged 8-15. The Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children-Fifth Edition (WISC-V), Clinical Assessment of Attention Deficit-Child (CAt-C), and Test of Everyday Attention for Children-Second Edition (TEA-Ch2) were selected as measures due to their established validity and reliability in assessing cognitive abilities, inattention symptoms, and attentional capacities, respectively, in this population. It was hypothesized that CTMT-2 performance would be significantly associated with cognitive abilities as measured by the WISC-V, inattention ratings on the CAt-C, and attention presentation scores on the TEA-Ch2. CTMT-2 performance was analyzed using correlation and multiple regression analyses with age and student grade levels, WISC-V cognitive abilities, CAt-C Inattention Rating scores, and TEA-Ch2 composite scores. The study yielded significant correlations between CTMT-2 performance and WISC-V Processing Speed Index (PSI) scores and TEA-Ch2 Selective Attention Index and Everyday Attention Index scores. Multiple regression analyses indicated that CTMT-2 trails significantly predicted PSI scores, TEA-Ch2 Selective Attention Index scores, and TEA-Ch2 Everyday Attention Index scores. The findings support the CTMT-2's utility in assessing selective and everyday attention and processing speed abilities in a sample of school-aged children and adolescents. These results have important implications for clinical practice, suggesting that the CTMT-2 can be a valuable supplemental tool in the comprehensive assessment of attentional deficits in this population. Future research should explore the CTMT-2's utility in diverse clinical samples and its potential to inform targeted interventions for children and adolescents with attentional difficulties.
