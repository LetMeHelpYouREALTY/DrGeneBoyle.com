Eugene Boyle, Ph.D. writes at the intersection of **clinical psychology** and **investor decision-making**—how attention, emotion, and habit shape what people do with real capital under uncertainty. The focus is not stock tips or market forecasts. It is the cognitive–behavioral side of investing: noticing the pull to sell winners too early, hold losers too long, or confuse a lucky outcome with a good process—and practicing a steadier response when something real is at stake.

Every brain arrives with wiring that works against wealth. Loss aversion makes losses sting more than equivalent gains feel good. Disposition effects push people to lock in the good feeling and postpone registering pain. Hindsight bias rewrites the past so the outcome looks inevitable. Knowing those patterns on paper rarely changes them. Feeling the urge in a live decision—and overriding it—is what begins to rewire the instinct. That practice sits squarely in a cognitive–behavioral tradition: observe the thought and feeling, test it against the evidence, choose the next action on purpose.

Self-directed markets are one of the few widely available arenas where ordinary people get rapid, consequential feedback on those skills. Financial literacy, mental budgeting, and self-control each improve investment choices and broader financial well-being; better decision-making is the bridge between having those skills and feeling more secure. The education from skin-in-the-game pays dividends whether an account beats a benchmark this year or not—calibrated judgment under uncertainty, comfort with risk, and fluency instead of fear.

### Clinical research that informs the lens

His Fielding Graduate University Ph.D. dissertation (2024-12-02; author credit: Boyle, Eugene) investigated the utility of the [Comprehensive Trail Making Test-Second Edition (CTMT-2)](/blog/ctmt-attention-trail-making-dissertation) for assessing attentional deficits among children and adolescents ([ProQuest 31761032](https://www.proquest.com/openview/0c70556dac0d45f933c90922209e53c8/1.pdf?cbl=18750&diss=y&pq-origsite=gscholar); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387076027_Investigating_the_Utility_of_the_Comprehensive_Trail_Making_Test_-Second_Edition_CTMT-2_in_Assessing_Attentional_Deficits_Among_Children_and_Adolescents)). He is also a co-author of [*Motherhood reimagined: Experiences of women with SMI surrounding parenting*](https://doi.org/10.1037/prj0000339) (*Psychiatric Rehabilitation Journal*, 2019), from qualitative research conducted under Lauren Mizock, Ph.D., at Fielding.

Clinical training includes a Ph.D. and M.A. in Clinical Psychology (Fielding), a forensic psychology internship at Heartland Behavioral Healthcare (competency and NGRI-related assessment), and memberships in APA, APS, and **ABCT** (Association for Behavioral and Cognitive Therapies)—the professional home for cognitive–behavioral science he brings to how people think, feel, and act around money and risk.
