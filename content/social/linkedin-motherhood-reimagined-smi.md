These 5 research themes changed how I talk about motherhood and serious mental illness.
(Sharing the framework from our published study)

Most coverage of women with SMI only talks about risk.

Custody loss. Poverty. Violence. Homelessness.

Those risks are real.
But they are not the whole story.

In 2019, Lauren Mizock, Andrea Laurel Merg, Elysse Kompaniez-Dunigan, and I published qualitative findings in *Psychiatric Rehabilitation Journal*.

We interviewed 20 women with SMI — with and without children.
Grounded theory. Five themes:

↳ Motherhood declined
↳ Motherhood derailed
↳ Motherhood disabled
↳ Motherhood reimagined
↳ Mattering through motherhood

That last one matters.
Motherhood isn’t only a risk profile.
For many women, it’s identity. Meaning. Feeling like they matter.

If you support women with SMI — clinically or in community — here’s the shortcut:

1. Ask about parenting goals without assuming the answer
2. Center self-determination (her values lead)
3. Map the resources she’d need if she wants kids — or wants a different path
4. Make room for grief when plans get derailed
5. Honor “reimagined” motherhood, not only one script
